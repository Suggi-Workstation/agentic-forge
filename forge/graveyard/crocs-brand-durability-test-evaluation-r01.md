## Findings

### The complete current rule makes the published positive result unsupported

The current framework requires five to ten years including weak conditions,
independent corroboration, real-price and volume separation, returns after
necessary reinvestment, a named threat, N/A for unsupported dimensions, and
`Unclear` before all other classes when conclusion-critical evidence is missing
or materially contradictory.[3] The company template separately requires the
mechanism, force, dimension, confidence, limitation, source, and consistency
checks; supported limited or uncertain conclusions pass its hard gate.[3]

The current Crocs report acknowledges two historical demand slumps, mixed
pricing power, thin independent demand evidence, Low confidence, and fashion
durability as its competence limit, yet assigns Durability 3, Trend 3, and
`Narrow / stable`.[3] That combination is inconsistent with the rule's ordered
classification. The target's `3/3/N/A/N/A, Unclear / indeterminate, Low` result
is the supported current-rule application: brand preference and recent segment
economics support Protection 3 and Economic proof 3, while missing retention,
constant-product pricing, elasticity, comparable share, brand invested capital,
and a current-business adverse cycle prevent numeric Durability and Trend
ratings.[1][3][16]

This supports a Crocs report correction. It does not show that the evidence
bridge, a new field, or a framework clause was needed to reach the correction.
The current contract states the decisive controls directly.[1][2][3]

### The annual issuer bridge and weak-period record reproduce

The SEC filings reproduce the target's material annual series. Crocs Brand
revenue was USD2,659.125 million, USD3,012.954 million, USD3,277.967 million,
and USD3,325.807 million in 2022-2025. Footwear units were 115.558 million,
119.577 million, 127.000 million, and 128.938 million; nominal ASP was USD22.72,
USD24.92, USD25.52, and USD25.40.[4][6][7]

Crocs Brand gross margin moved from 56.3% to 60.0%, 61.6%, and 61.3%.
Segment operating income was USD852.025 million, USD1,079.330 million,
USD1,182.012 million, and USD1,111.679 million.[4][6][7] Independent Decimal
recalculation gives operating margins of 32.041555%, 35.822983%, 36.059301%,
and 33.425842%, agreeing with the target after rounding. North American revenue
fell 6.751692% in 2025 while international revenue rose 11.881042%; total brand
revenue rose 1.459441% while segment operating income fell 5.950278%.[1][4]

The issuer also says the 2025 Crocs Brand ASP decline reduced revenue by
USD10.2 million and was driven mostly by increased discounting, partly offset
by channel mix. Duties and freight or fulfillment outweighed lower product
cost, and segment operating income fell 6.0%.[4] The figures establish strong
recent economics followed by weaker price realization and margins. Because ASP
mixes product, channel, geography, and discounts, they do not establish
constant-product real pricing power.[3][4][16]

The weak-period direction also reproduces. The 2009 filing reports the 2008
demand contraction, inventory liquidation, model-mix change, and impaired
inventory effects that make 2009 an unsuitable clean pricing observation.[8]
The 2016 filing reports operating losses of USD4.726 million, USD72.324 million,
and USD6.154 million in 2014-2016; 2015 units rose 3.7% while gross margin fell
to 46.8%, and 2016 units and ASP fell 3.7% and 1.7%.[9] Changed products,
channels, and segment definitions limit direct comparison, but the periods are
valid contrary evidence against automatic durability.[1][8][9]

### One current-quarter source statement is wrong but does not reverse the result

The target correctly replaces the current report's Q2 2026 channel figures with
GAAP Crocs Brand changes of DTC +12.9% and wholesale -5.0%; it also reproduces
North America +0.4%, international +7.8%, and total brand revenue +4.3%.[5]
The release still omits Crocs Brand quarterly units and ASP, so it cannot isolate
constant-product price, volume, mix, channel, and geography.[5]

The target is wrong, however, when it says the release does not disclose a
Crocs Brand margin. The official release gives Q2 GAAP Crocs Brand gross margin
of 62.6% versus 64.1% and adjusted gross margin of 63.1% versus 64.1%.[5] The
59.4% versus 61.7% figures cited by the target are consolidated GAAP gross
margin. Any Crocs report correction must preserve the disclosed brand gross-
margin decline and narrow the remaining quarterly gaps to units, ASP, operating
margin, and price-volume attribution.

This error lowers confidence in the target's source-completeness claim but does
not require a corrective research cycle for the root decision. The omitted
brand margin is contrary to the target's availability statement and directionally
supports, rather than resolves, the uncertain durability and trend result. It
also supplies no unique benefit for the proposed bridge over direct application
of the current source and framework.[1][3][5]

### The consumer record is bounded and does not prove durability or erosion

Native text and rendered-page checks of the official Piper tables reproduce the
all-teen series. Crocs was No. 6 at a rounded 1% in Fall 2021 and Spring 2022,
No. 5 at 2% in Fall 2022, and No. 6 at 2% in Spring and Fall 2023.[10][11]
The Fall 2025 public infographic shows only Nike 46%, Adidas 14%, and New
Balance 8% in its displayed top-footwear list. Crocs is absent from the displayed
three, but the page does not report all lower ranks; absence is not a zero or a
measured decline.[12]

The broader observations are also real but answer different questions.
AlixPartners surveyed 9,000 consumers in July 2024 across access, experience,
service, product, and price and ranked Crocs first among listed fashion-footwear
players; Crocs led the displayed Gen Z and Millennial choices at 25% and 29%.[13]
StockX ranked Crocs its No. 2 best-selling shoe brand in 2024 for the second
straight year on its own resale marketplace.[14] YouGov's Q4 2025 U.S. ranking
shows Crocs at 94% fame and 51% popularity; its FAQ says the sample is weighted
to be nationally representative and each quarterly website update uses the
preceding 90 days.[15]

These sources contradict a claim of broad current rejection. They do not measure
repeat purchase, retention, Crocs-specific market share, price elasticity,
constant-product willingness to pay, or ten-year protection. The target is
sound to keep them separate and to reject a composite durability score.[1][3]

### The package bytes reproduce, but the access boundary receives no extra weight

Independent extraction reproduced all four declared records exactly: the
4,055-byte dispatch receipt, 14,540-byte evidence pack, 17,529-byte Reader A
output, and 13,645-byte Reader B output match their four stated SHA-256 values.
Both outputs apply the complete result schema and independently return
`3/3/N/A/N/A, Unclear / indeterminate, Low`.[1]

The preserved dispatch receipt is a parent-written record of the prompts and
returned summaries, while the output access receipts are reader self-reports.
The artifact does not preserve complete delegated tool transcripts that would
independently prove the claimed no-other-source boundary. Reader isolation and
agreement therefore receive no additional evidentiary weight.[1][17] Reader B's
efficient-scale row also cites the frozen Circana annual figures that the target
later marks unverified. That row receives zero weight; named funded rivals and
the contestable footwear market independently support the same mechanism result,
and efficient scale does not decide the ordered `Unclear` classification.[1][3]

Neither limitation blocks disposition. The evaluator directly checked the exact
current framework, current report, primary issuer tables, survey tables,
material calculations, and contrary sources. Those checks independently support
the current-rule result and the zero incremental-method conclusion without
relying on procedural reader agreement.[1][3][4][5][10][11]

### Correct application changes the report but leaves no reusable method increment

The strongest support for further work is material: the published company report
contains unsupported Durability and Trend ratings, an unsupported positive moat
class, two mechanism absences that should remain Unknown, and wrong Q2 channel
figures.[1][3][5] Doing nothing is not supported. A report-only correction to
`3/3/N/A/N/A, Unclear / indeterminate, Low`, corrected channel and margin facts,
and conditional valuation-persistence wording is the smallest evidence-based
response.[1]

The contrary and decisive evidence is that the full current framework already
forces every semantic change. The 14,540-byte bridge and detailed reader outputs
show the application more visibly, but no comparison demonstrates a unique
mechanism, dimension, class, trend, confidence, valuation, thesis, or trigger
change beyond correct current-rule use. No prospective report was tested, no
smaller bridge was compared with an equally informed full-contract reader, and
no complete author plus reviewer time was measured.[1][2][3]

A reusable bridge, framework clarification, or template addition is therefore
unsupported. A REVISE would ask for evidence whose absence does not block this
negative incremental-method result. A REFRAME would repeat the same current-
rule-sufficiency question without changed evidence. The report application
error is a domain correction for an authorized Investing Hub review, not proof
that this Forge method candidate should advance.[1][2][3]

## Verdict and Handoff

**Verdict: REJECT. Exact next stage: closed in `forge/graveyard/`. Zero of two
corrective cycles were used; this terminal verdict consumes no corrective
cycle.**

The research is grounded enough to decide the root question. Primary issuer
records, rendered survey tables, independent calculations, and the current
contract support `3/3/N/A/N/A, Unclear / indeterminate, Low` and expose a
material Crocs report application error. The same checks show that the existing
contract reaches that result before the candidate evidence bridge is treated as
a reusable method. The root idea's current-rule and zero-increment stop
condition is met.[1][2][3]

The exact handoff is closure. Remove only pipeline `20261001T103830Z` from
`STATUS.md`. No proposal, Investing Hub report edit, framework or template
change, implementation, security recommendation, or approval is authorized.
The verified Q2 brand-margin omission must be corrected if the company report is
later reviewed, but it does not create a bridge-specific decision benefit.[5][17]

Reopening requires changed evidence: a prospectively predeclared report sample
in which faithful application of the complete current contract misses a material
moat error uniquely caught by a bounded bridge, or measured evidence that a
smaller retained record lowers total author and reviewer cost without adding
false confidence. Changed primary evidence that resolves retention, real price,
elasticity, comparable share, segment returns, and an adverse current-business
cycle could instead justify reassessing Crocs itself, but it would not by itself
prove a new method is needed.[1][2][3]

Confidence in REJECT is medium. Confidence is high in the current-rule ordered
classification, the reproduced annual and weak-period facts, the Piper table
limits, and the absence of a measured unique method benefit.[3][4][6][7][8][9]
Overall confidence remains medium because conclusion-critical durability data
are unavailable, the readers share one model family, their complete access
transcripts are not preserved, and the target missed a disclosed Q2 Crocs Brand
gross-margin series.[1][5] Confidence would fall if a faithful current-rule
application supported the published positive result or passed a material error
that the bridge alone detected. It would rise if a prospective predeclared
comparison reproduced the zero increment with complete burden records.
