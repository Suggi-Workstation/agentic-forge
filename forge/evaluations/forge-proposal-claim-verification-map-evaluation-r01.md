## Findings

### The reported row counts and answer-key classifications are internally correct

An independent parse of the target found 56 consecutively identified rows:
19 for history-receipt r01, 21 for history-receipt r02, and 16 for the
bounded-freshness control. The aggregate was 50 `COMPLETE`, one `ABSENT`, four
`CONTRADICTORY`, and one `UNVERIFIABLE`, matching the report's table.[1]

Direct comparison with the exact negative proposals and later reviews supports
the six classifications. R01 places a body-producing existence probe before its
byte admission and lacks a running-command deadline. R02 measures possible
excess body output after the sink has accepted it, gates Git children but not
later in-process work, records command events but not every oracle-relevant
in-process operation, and rejects shallow clones without covering partial or
promisor behavior. The corresponding reviews name the same two and four
blockers.[3]

The 16-row READY control maps its trigger, state comparisons, wait, second
freshness call, cleanup, prohibited producer actions, acceptance matrix, limits,
and rollback to observable future checks. The proposal's permission-before-
cleanup wording needs the implementation-conformance interpretation recorded by
its READY review, but the acceptance row requires zero query calls after cleanup
failure. The research therefore did not convert that wording into a false design
blocker.[3]

These checks support the bounded conclusion that the displayed map can encode
the known answer-key defects and the selected positive control. NASA's current
verification guidance also supports unique requirement identity, a named
verification approach, per-requirement acceptance criteria, recorded results,
and bidirectional traceability. It does not validate this Forge instrument or
its effect on authors.[6][7]

### The claimed frozen instrument and separate-reader reproduction are not independently checkable

The target supplies a SHA-256 value for a 279-word frozen instrument but does
not include or link the exact instrument bytes whose hash was calculated. Its
prose lists seven fields and classification rules, but no preserved byte range is
identified as the hashed object. A repository-wide check found the hash only in
the target report. A hash without available bytes establishes no independently
checkable instrument identity.[1][4]

The target displays one set of 56 filled rows. It says that a separate reader
saved proposal-only maps before opening each review and later reproduced every
classification, but it preserves only the agreement summary, two elapsed values,
and two disputed-label explanations. It does not preserve that reader's separate
row inventory, classifications, source ranges, disagreements, or pre-answer-key
output. No separate source artifact preserves that reader's row inventory or
timed output.[1]

This blocks the report's reproducibility and grouping claims. The existing Forge
lesson explicitly requires exact instrument bytes, raw per-case outputs, and
each reader's separate output before treating a procedural comparison as
reproducible. Brain prior work independently reaches the same boundary: a check
proves only what it could distinguish, a trace proves only what its
instrumentation emits, and an evidence ledger must preserve separate claims,
statuses, and reviewer reasoning.[4][5]

The missing records do not show that the classifications are wrong. They show
that the independent-reader result and complete-claim-grouping result are
unknown from durable evidence.

### The checklist comparison does not establish incremental detection

The current proposal template's eight checklist items are semantic gates. They
require an evidence-supported implementable scope, sufficient implementation
detail, resolved blockers, and acceptance, regression, negative, worst-case,
and reversal checks. They do not define PASS as the presence of eight sections
or topics.[2]

The research applied the checklist after the claim maps and reports only that
all three proposals represented 8 of 8 items; from that presence check it assigns
the checklist 0 of 6 detections. It provides no predeclared checklist decision
rule, filled per-proposal checklist, classified finding, separate-reader output,
or positive-control false-blocker result. It also does not show how a reader
using the checklist's full feasibility and observable-test wording would have
classified the same operation-order defects.[1][2]

That is not a fair comparison between the proposed map and the existing gate.
The broad checklist may still be less consistent or less usable than the map,
but the reported 6/6 versus 0/6 contrast does not establish that incremental
value. Source-verification practice likewise requires comparison rules, exact
claims, separate evidence states, and reviewer records; a result label cannot
substitute for those records.[5]

### The scope and evidence limits remain appropriate

The report correctly labels the study retrospective, the answer keys disclosed,
the two negative revisions one pipeline, the positive control one pipeline, and
the separate reader the same model family. It does not claim fewer future
revisions or measured author benefit, rejects an executable checker, and keeps
implementation separately authorized.[1]

A prospective trial is not required to repair the retrospective comparison if
the conclusion remains limited to retrospective diagnostic value. It is required
before claiming causal reduction in future review defects, author time, or net
benefit. The revised research should also distinguish the 279-word rubric from
the burden of completing 56 rows; one reader time and one freeze-to-comparison
interval do not measure authoring burden for both compared methods.[1][6]

## Verdict and Handoff

**Verdict: REVISE. Exact next stage: `research`. This verdict uses corrective
cycle 1 of 2.**

The research provides a credible retrospective signal: its displayed map matches
all six disclosed blockers and does not falsely block the selected READY control.
It does not yet support advancement because the exact frozen instrument and
separate-reader outputs are unavailable, and the presence-only checklist baseline
cannot establish incremental detection over the current semantic gate.[1][2][3]

The revised research must:

1. Embed or link the exact frozen instrument bytes and verify their hash. Preserve
   the first reader's and separate reader's outputs as distinct records, including
   inputs, row inventories, source ranges, classifications, timestamps or elapsed
   measures, disagreements, and the point at which each answer key was opened.
   If the original bytes or reader output no longer exist, run a new predeclared
   comparison and label it a new pass rather than reconstructing the old one.
2. State whether the separate reader independently extracts the complete claim
   inventory or only classifies a supplied inventory. For independent extraction,
   compare omissions, grouping, splits, merges, and unclassified claims before
   reconciling labels. Otherwise narrow the reproducibility claim to classification
   agreement on a shared row set.
3. Freeze a semantic application rule for the current proposal checklist before
   grading. Apply it to the same two negative revisions and READY control under the
   same answer-key and reader boundary, preserving filled per-target outputs and
   false-blocker results. Do not equate section or item presence with checklist
   PASS.
4. Report the comparison burden separately for each method and reader: rubric
   words, filled-output rows and words, completion time, disagreements,
   unclassified claims, detected blockers, and false blockers. Keep prospective
   revision reduction and authoring benefit unknown unless a separately designed
   prospective control measures them.

A fresh evaluation may ADVANCE only if durable records support the grouping and
classification claim, the full-contract checklist comparison still shows
meaningful incremental detection, and the map remains a proportionate manual
proposal gate. If the fair baseline closes the apparent advantage or independent
claim extraction is not reproducible, the next evaluation should narrow,
reframe, or stop the idea rather than force a proposal.

Confidence in REVISE is high. The six displayed classifications are supported by
the exact proposal/review text, while the absent instrument bytes, absent
separate-reader record, and presence-only comparator are direct properties of the
research artifact and current template.[1][2][3][4] Confidence would fall if a
linked immutable record supplied the exact instrument and unmerged reader outputs
and a predeclared semantic checklist comparison reproduced the claimed
incremental result. No such record is linked or present in the current Forge
corpus.

## Learning Decision

Strengthen the existing high-confidence lesson, `Preserve procedural validation
evidence before comparison`; do not add a new lesson or raise its confidence.[4]

- **Selection:** The idea remains worth testing. The blocker concerns comparison
  evidence, not the known answer-key classifications or the value of making hard
  proposal claims observable.
- **Evidence and test design:** Exact first-reader rows made the six classifications
  checkable, while a hash without instrument bytes, summary-only second-reader
  agreement, and a presence-only semantic-checklist baseline left reproduction
  and incremental value uncheckable.
- **Process:** The target repeated an existing evidence-preservation failure even
  though the current lesson already named exact instruments, raw per-case output,
  and separate reader output. The method should state that every compared method,
  including the baseline, needs its full-contract application rule and separate
  output.
- **Repetition:** This is a new pipeline but not a new lesson class. It supplies an
  independent case of the existing preservation failure and shows that comparator
  evidence must not be reduced to an underpowered surrogate.
- **Coverage:** Updating the existing lesson with the pipeline and comparator
  consequence is non-duplicative, uses checked evidence, leaves high confidence
  unchanged, and grants no governance or implementation authority.
