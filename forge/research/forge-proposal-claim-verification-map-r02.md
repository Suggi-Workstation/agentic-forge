# Revised Research: Forge Proposal Claim-to-Verification Map

## Question and Method

This revision tests the same narrow question as r01: does a compact claim-to-
verification map expose known proposal defects better than the current proposal
checklist, without falsely blocking a READY control? It answers the r01
evaluation's four correction requests: exact instrument preservation, unmerged
reader records, independent claim extraction, a full semantic checklist
baseline, and method-specific burden.[1]

The provisional explanation, recorded after reading the root, r01 research,
evaluation, current template, and exact target chain but before the new reader
passes, was that the original 6/6-versus-0/6 result probably conflated a semantic
map with an item-presence baseline. A full-contract checklist reading might find
the same operation-order, resource-bound, observability, and no-side-effect
defects. The map could still add a reviewable evidence ledger, but that would be
a different benefit from incremental detection. Disconfirmation conditions were
zero incremental detections, divergent independently extracted inventories,
unsupported blockers, or disproportionate record burden.[1][2][3]

A new 607-word, 4,502-byte comparison instrument was frozen before either
reader graded a target. Its SHA-256 is
`e192e8610c0942afcd1cad868920bb307d86efe1cf01825d3213d86e2677d37a`.
It defines one row per independently extracted material claim or grouped
invariant, the seven map fields and four classes, and a semantic reading of all
eight existing checklist items. In particular, checklist C5 tests whether the
normative sequence can enforce every material behavior, bound, operation order,
external-state limit, and no-side-effect claim; C6 tests observable positive,
negative, regression, consuming-boundary, and prohibited-later-action evidence.
The exact instrument bytes appear in Appendix A.[2][4]

Two isolated readers used the configured `gpt-5.6-sol` model family. Reader A
applied the map to all three proposals before the checklist; Reader B used the
reverse order. Each independently extracted its map inventory from the full
proposal bodies. Neither received the r01 map's row inventory. Both saved and
SHA-256-hashed their proposal-only map and checklist records before opening any
paired final review, and neither edited those records afterward. The later
review pass only matched the frozen findings to the disclosed answer keys. This
is order-balanced same-family replication, not model independence or a blind
trial.[1][3][4]

The three unchanged targets were the two failed history-receipt revisions and
the READY bounded-freshness control. The six later review blockers and READY
verdict were disclosed as answer keys, but their bodies were withheld from each
reader until its proposal-only outputs were frozen. Exact proposal, review,
template, and reader-output hashes appear in Appendices B through I. The Forge
hybrid index was fresh and returned the root, r01 research, and evaluation as its
top three relevant records; those files were read in full. A fresh Brain query
returned verification, evidence-ledger, systems-engineering, observability, and
measurement sources, which were also read in full. NASA's current verification
matrix and product-verification guidance were read as primary practice sources.
They support traceability and preserved evidence; they do not validate this
local instrument or its incremental value.[5][6][7][8]

At starting HEAD `b1ba233a57ea387e94e2418bac3e064d78164fe7`, the worktree
was clean. The board had one valid unselected human-review row and the selected
pipeline at `research`, with the exact r01 evaluation as input. The root idea was
authored by Analyst, so research-author independence passed. Progress ended at
ENT-020, errors at ENT-026, and corrective cycle 1 of 2 was already used. The
board, logs, HEAD, and read-only `LEARNINGS.md` remained byte-identical through
the pre-write gate.[9]

## Evidence and Findings

### Exact records repair the preservation failure

The proposal-only outputs and post-review match records are embedded separately
in Appendices B through I. Independent parsing verified all eight JSON records,
their hashes, and the reported row totals. No hash stands in for unavailable
bytes in this revision.[1][4]

| Reader and order | Method | Rows | Preserved output words | Method elapsed | Disclosed blockers detected | READY false blockers |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| A: map then checklist | Map | 60 | 5,715 | 178 s | 4 of 6 | 0 |
| A: map then checklist | Checklist | 24 | 756 | 10 s | 4 of 6 | 0 |
| B: checklist then map | Checklist | 24 | 689 | 175 s | 5 of 6 | 0 |
| B: checklist then map | Map | 79 | 6,423 | 271 s | 5 of 6 | 0 |

The word counts are whitespace-delimited counts of the preserved JSON, including
record labels. The elapsed values are reader-reported wall-clock intervals for
each method output. They are not authoring-time measurements. Reader A's
10-second checklist pass followed its full map and can reuse understanding;
Reader B's 271-second map followed its checklist. The counterbalanced order
makes that carryover visible but two same-family readers cannot estimate a clean
method-time effect.[4]

### Independent extraction did not reproduce one complete claim inventory

Reader A extracted 60 map rows; Reader B extracted 79. Both reported zero
unclassified claims, yet the 19-row difference shows that `complete claim
inventory` was not operationally stable under the frozen grouping rule. Their
pre-review blocker inventories also differed:

- Reader A identified both r01 blockers, the r02 emitted-byte defect, and the
  r02 command-only trace defect. It missed the r02 in-process deadline and
  partial-clone blockers. Its map also produced three blocker rows unsupported
  by the paired reviews: pre-deadline prerequisites, non-byte-safe path
  enumeration, and root-limit observation order.
- Reader B identified both r01 blockers, the r02 emitted-byte defect,
  in-process deadline defect, and partial-clone defect. It missed the r02
  command-only trace defect. Its map also produced two blocker rows unsupported
  by the paired review for that target: an r01 partial-clone finding and an r02
  runtime-generated-driver conformance finding.

Only three of the six disclosed blockers were independently detected by both
readers. Their union covered all six. This is useful evidence that repeated
reading can improve recall, but it is evidence against the stronger claim that
the map makes complete claim extraction reproducible. Same-family agreement on
some classifications does not remove grouping and omission variance.[3][4][6]

### A fair semantic checklist matched the map's detection set

Within each reader, the map and semantic checklist detected exactly the same
set of disclosed blockers:

| Reader | Map result | Checklist result | Map-only detections | Checklist-only detections |
|:--|:--|:--|--:|--:|
| A | 4 of 6 | 4 of 6 | 0 | 0 |
| B | 5 of 6 | 5 of 6 | 0 | 0 |

Reader A's checklist failed C5 and C6 on each negative proposal and passed all
eight items on the READY control. Reader B produced the same item-level results.
The full-contract C5/C6 reading found the defects that each reader had already
recognized in the map: infeasible or incorrectly ordered controls under C5 and
missing observability or negative fixtures under C6. The map supplied finer
claim-level localization, but it supplied no incremental blocker detection in
either frozen reader pass.[2][3]

The original r01 conclusion that the map detected 6/6 while the checklist
detected 0/6 therefore does not survive the corrected baseline. Item presence
was not the checklist's contract. The current template requires a feasible,
detailed specification and observable acceptance, regression, negative,
worst-case, and reversal checks. Applying those clauses semantically can expose
material counterexamples without a separate map.[1][2]

Neither method produced a false blocker on the READY control. Both readers
recognized its cleanup-before-query wording as an implementation-conformance
condition because the proposal's acceptance contract forbids a query after
cleanup failure and the READY review preserves that interpretation. This result
supports specificity on one control; it does not estimate false-blocker rates on
other proposal classes.[3]

### The map preserved more detail but at materially larger record size

The map produced 60 and 79 rows compared with 24 fixed checklist rows, and
5,715 and 6,423 preserved words compared with 756 and 689. Those exact counts
establish record burden, not economic cost. The map's row-level source ranges,
operation order, predicates, controls, evidence state, and class make its
reasoning easier to audit after the fact. The checklist compresses several
related defects into C5 and C6 findings, making it shorter but less granular.

The experiment did not measure whether map granularity lowers later reviewer
time, improves correction quality, or prevents omissions prospectively. It also
did not compare independent human or different-model authors, nonprocedural
proposals, or proposals without disclosed answer keys. A mandatory map cannot be
justified from record detail alone when detection did not improve and extraction
diverged.[4][5][6]

### External and Brain evidence supports the method boundary, not the local result

NASA's example matrix gives each mandatory requirement a unique identity, source,
and verification approach. Its product-verification guidance calls for per-
requirement acceptance criteria, recorded methods and results, objective
evidence, and bidirectional traceability.[7][8] Brain systems-engineering and
source-verification work independently support claim decomposition, evidence
ledgers, traceability, and review records, while observability work warns that a
trace proves only the operations its instrumentation emits.[5][6]

Those sources explain why a map can be useful as a review record. They do not
show that every Forge proposal needs one, that its inventory will be complete,
or that it detects more than the current semantic checklist. Brain measurement
work further distinguishes repeatability from reproducibility: changed
conditions must be named, and same-method agreement does not test independent
error mechanisms. The present same-family readers therefore support a bounded
within-sample comparison only.[6]

## Alternatives and Implications

| Alternative | Evidence in this revision | Implication |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Keep the current semantic checklist | Each reader's full-contract checklist detected exactly the same blocker set as its map, with 24 rows and no READY false blocker.[2][3] | Simplest supported baseline. It still depends on disciplined semantic application and did not make either reader complete. |
| Strengthen checklist execution without adding a map | The template already contains the decisive C5/C6 clauses, but r01 compared only item presence.[1][2] | A smaller procedural clarification may improve application, but this research did not test any wording change prospectively. |
| Require the full map | It preserved finer evidence and source ranges, but added no detection, produced 60/79 divergent rows, and emitted unsupported blockers.[3][4] | Not supported as a mandatory proposal gate by this sample. |
| Restrict mapping to hard bounds, operation order, prohibited actions, and no-side-effect claims | Those categories contain all six disclosed blockers and would be smaller than a complete claim inventory.[3] | Plausible reframe, not tested here; it needs a new predeclared comparison rather than being inferred as successful. |
| Build an executable checker | Semantic claim extraction and grouping remained reader-dependent; a prior checker was deferred after a real-record false rejection.[1][4] | Outside scope and unsupported. |
| Do nothing | Avoids new ceremony and retains independent final review.[2][9] | Accepts reader-dependent misses; the evidence does not show whether a template clarification would improve them. |

The evidence does not support advancing the current hypothesis into a mandatory
map proposal. It supports a narrower finding: a map is an auditable evidence
ledger, but the existing checklist can detect the same known blockers when read
semantically, and neither method made one same-family reader complete. Evaluation
should decide whether to stop the idea or reframe it around a narrower claim
category or reviewability outcome; research does not issue that verdict.

No decision-critical evidence gap blocks evaluation of this retrospective
negative result. Prospective defect reduction, authoring cost, reviewer cost,
nonprocedural false blockers, different-model extraction, and the value of a
narrow map remain unknown. They are prerequisites for stronger benefit claims,
not reasons to restore the invalid 6/6-versus-0/6 comparison.

## Response to Feedback and Remaining Questions

1. **Exact instrument and separate reader evidence:** Appendix A preserves the
   complete frozen instrument. Appendices B through I preserve each reader's map,
   checklist, post-review match, and metadata record separately. The proposal-
   only hashes were created before reviews were opened and independently
   rechecked. The records include inputs, line ranges, inventories, classes,
   method order, timestamps, elapsed values, word counts, disagreements, answer-
   key boundary, and hashes.[1]
2. **Independent extraction and grouping:** Both readers independently extracted
   claims. Their inventories differed by 19 rows; only three disclosed blockers
   were common detections, and each reader missed a different subset. The claim
   is therefore narrowed from reproducible complete extraction to separately
   preserved, reader-dependent extraction.[1][4]
3. **Semantic checklist baseline:** The checklist rule was frozen before grading
   and applied as a full semantic contract. It found the same blocker set as the
   map within each reader, so the map's claimed incremental 6/6-versus-0/6
   advantage is withdrawn.[1][2]
4. **Burden:** The table reports rubric size, output rows and words, elapsed time,
   unclassified claims, detected blockers, unsupported blocker rows, and false
   blockers by reader and method. Timing remains order-confounded and is not
   represented as authoring cost or economic benefit.[1]

Remaining questions are bounded:

1. Would a map limited to hard bounds, required order, prohibited actions, and
   no-side-effect claims retain useful localization with materially fewer rows?
2. Would different-model or human readers still produce divergent inventories
   and complementary misses?
3. Does row-level evidence reduce independent final-review time or corrective
   cycles prospectively compared with an explicitly executed semantic checklist?
4. What false-blocker rate appears across descriptive, policy, and low-risk
   proposals rather than one READY procedural control?

Confidence is medium. Exact instrument bytes, unmerged records, counterbalanced
method order, independently extracted inventories, verified hashes, full-
contract checklist application, exact proposal/review comparisons, and zero
READY-control false blockers make the corrected retrospective result checkable.
Confidence is not high because there are only two same-family readers, two
negative revisions from one pipeline, one positive control, disclosed answer
keys, order carryover, and no prospective author or reviewer outcome. Confidence
would rise if different-model or human readers reproduce the no-increment result
on new proposals with preserved full records. It would fall if a proportionate
map detects decision-relevant defects that an equally explicit semantic
checklist repeatedly misses.

## Appendix A -- Exact Frozen Comparison Instrument

The bytes inside the following fence are the complete instrument whose SHA-256
is stated above.

```markdown
# Frozen Comparison Instrument

Frozen scope: independently apply both methods to these proposal bodies before opening their paired final reviews:

1. `forge/proposals/forge-history-duplicate-receipt-r01.md`
2. `forge/proposals/forge-history-duplicate-receipt-r02.md`
3. `forge/proposals/bounded-index-freshness-recheck-r02.md`

The first two are disclosed negative targets with two and four later review blockers. The third is a disclosed READY control. This is a retrospective diagnostic, not a blind trial. Do not infer prospective author benefit.

## Method M: claim-to-verification map

Independently inventory every material normative claim or grouped invariant in the proposal. Material claims prescribe scope, behavior, operation order, resource bounds, prohibited actions, external-state limits, acceptance evidence, rollback, or authority. Exclude descriptive rationale. Group claims only when they share one gate, observable result, and evidence state.

For every row record: unique reader row ID; exact proposal line range; concise claim; required operation and order (`O`); observable predicate (`P`); valid positive control (`+`); failure-directed negative case and prohibited later action (`-`); evidence state (`E`: performed, future, or unknown); and class.

Classify `COMPLETE` only when operation order, predicate, controls, prohibited later action, and evidence state are mutually consistent and sufficient to establish the claim. Classify `ABSENT` when a required element is missing, `CONTRADICTORY` when the specified sequence permits the claim to be violated, and `UNVERIFIABLE` when the proposed evidence cannot observe the claimed behavior. A material non-`COMPLETE` row is a blocker. Future evidence is not itself a blocker when the proposal specifies an executable and complete future acceptance contract. `N/A` requires a reason.

After saving the proposal-only output, open the paired review. Match each disclosed review blocker to a pre-review row without revising that row. Record unmatched blockers and pre-review blocker rows not supported by the review.

## Method C: current proposal checklist, semantic application

Apply all eight checklist items from `governance/template-proposal.md` to the complete proposal as semantic gates, not section-presence tests. Record one row per item and target. `PASS` requires the entire relevant contract to satisfy the item. `FAIL` requires an exact counterexample with proposal lines. `N/A` requires genuine inapplicability.

- C1: ordered metadata and exact evaluated-research/revision links satisfy protocol.
- C2: nonempty tags and justified low/medium/high confidence are present.
- C3: a matching ADVANCE evaluation exists and all evaluation blockers are resolved.
- C4: feedback, evidence, counterevidence, alternatives, and non-goals are substantively included.
- C5: the change specification is feasible and detailed enough to implement. Its normative sequence can actually enforce every material behavior, bound, operation order, external-state limit, and no-side-effect claim; dependencies and effective configuration scopes are covered.
- C6: observable acceptance, regression, and negative tests cover every material normative claim, including consuming-boundary order and prohibited later actions; worst plausible failure and reversal are specified.
- C7: future work is not represented as performed, approved, or deployed.
- C8: Sources follow the protocol format and support the claims made.

After saving the proposal-only output, open the paired review. A disclosed blocker is detected when a pre-review `FAIL` finding identifies the same defect and boundary. Record unmatched blockers and unsupported `FAIL` findings. On the READY control, a `FAIL` is a false blocker when the READY review does not support it and it cannot be resolved as an implementation-conformance condition already represented by the proposal.

## Reader and preservation rules

Use proposal line numbers from a full file read. Do not open any paired review until both proposal-only method outputs for that target are written. Never edit a proposal-only output after opening a review. Preserve method outputs separately. Record method order, UTC start/end, elapsed seconds, row count, output word count, disagreements, unclassified claims, detected blockers, and READY-control false blockers. State whether the claim inventory was independently extracted or supplied. Do not treat agreement by the same model family as model independence.

```
