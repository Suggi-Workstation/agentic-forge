# Research: Crocs Brand Durability Test

## Question and Method

This report tests the exact question in the root idea: does a source-aligned
Crocs Brand record support the current company report's `3/3/3/3, Narrow /
stable, Low` moat result under the unchanged `simple-moat.md` contract, and
does a reusable evidence-bridge method add decision value beyond correct use of
that contract?[1][2][3]

The assessed perimeter is the Crocs Brand, not HEYDUDE. The provisional
explanation before targeted evidence gathering was that Crocs has a real brand
advantage and strong recent economics, but that current margin and awareness do
not establish ten-year durability. The decisive gaps were constant-product
pricing power, retention, comparable share, adverse-cycle persistence, and the
comparability of one U.S. teen cohort. Disconfirmation would have been a
like-for-like record showing stable real price, volume, margin, and broad
consumer preference through weak conditions, or two cold applications of the
current rule reproducing the published `Narrow / stable` result.[1][2][24]

## Evidence and Findings

### The 2022-2025 record shows strong economics, then weaker price and margin

Amounts are USD millions except footwear units in thousands and nominal ASP in
USD per pair. Operating margin is derived as Crocs Brand operating income divided
by Crocs Brand revenue.

| Year | Revenue | Footwear units | Nominal ASP | Gross margin | Operating income | Derived operating margin |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2022 | 2,659.125 | 115,558 | 22.72 | 56.3% | 852.025 | 32.0416% |
| 2023 | 3,012.954 | 119,577 | 24.92 | 60.0% | 1,079.330 | 35.8230% |
| 2024 | 3,277.967 | 127,000 | 25.52 | 61.6% | 1,182.012 | 36.0593% |
| 2025 | 3,325.807 | 128,938 | 25.40 | 61.3% | 1,111.679 | 33.4258% |

The 2023 filing attributes the 9.7% nominal-ASP increase to product mix,
increased pricing, lower discounting, and channel mix; units rose 3.5% and gross
margin rose 370 basis points. The 2024 filing reports 6.2% unit growth and a 2.4%
ASP increase, with volume, channel mix, selected international price increases,
and product mix contributing; gross margin rose 160 basis points.[6][7]

The direction changed in 2025. Units rose 1.5%, but nominal ASP fell 0.5%.
Crocs says increased discounting, partly offset by channel mix, reduced Crocs
Brand revenue by USD10.2 million. Gross margin fell 30 basis points as duties and
freight/fulfillment outweighed lower product cost. Operating income fell 6.0%,
and derived operating margin fell 263.3 basis points.[5]

This is evidence of profitable brand preference, not isolated pricing power.
The issuer's ASP is footwear and footwear-accessory revenue divided by units and
therefore mixes product, channel, geography, and discounts. The 2023 definition
also refers to footwear and charms, while the 2025 filing refers to footwear and
footwear accessories. No constant-product or real-price series was found.[5][7]

### Growth was not broad across geography and channel

| Year | North America | International | Wholesale | DTC |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 2023 | 1,777.885 | 1,235.069 | 1,493.537 | 1,519.417 |
| 2024 | 1,833.422 | 1,444.545 | 1,607.546 | 1,670.421 |
| 2025 | 1,709.635 | 1,616.172 | 1,599.374 | 1,726.433 |

In 2025, North America fell 6.75% while international rose 11.88%; wholesale
fell 0.5% while DTC rose 3.4%. The 1.5% total brand growth therefore did not mean
broad demand growth.[5]

Q2 2026 Crocs Brand revenue rose 4.3% to USD1,000.437 million. DTC rose 12.9%,
wholesale fell 5.0%, North America rose 0.4%, and international rose 7.8%.
Consolidated gross margin fell from 61.7% to 59.4%; the release does not disclose
Crocs Brand units, ASP, or brand-specific margin. It therefore cannot establish
that price, rather than volume, mix, channel, or geography, protected the
quarter.[10] This also corrects the current report's 12.0% DTC and negative 7.2%
wholesale statement, which is not the Q2 2026 Crocs Brand channel table.[3]

### Weak periods contradict automatic durability

The 2009 filing reports that 2008 company revenue fell to USD721.589 million from
USD847.350 million in 2007, gross margin fell from 58.7% to 32.4%, and operating
loss was USD188.282 million. In 2009, footwear units rose from 35.3 million to
36.8 million while revenue still declined as Crocs liquidated discontinued and
impaired inventory. The filing says sales of impaired inventory generated
USD58.3 million of revenue and an unusual USD49.8 million positive gross-profit
effect, so 2009 is not a clean price/margin observation.[8]

The pre-HEYDUDE company record is close to, but not identical with, the current
Crocs Brand perimeter. Revenue fell 9.0% in 2015 even as units rose 3.7%; nominal
ASP was USD18.53, gross margin fell to 46.8%, and operating loss was USD72.324
million. The filing names lower-priced mix, retail weakness, and increased
fourth-quarter discounting. In 2016 revenue fell 5.0%, units fell 3.7%, ASP fell
1.7% to USD18.21, and operating loss was USD6.154 million.[9]

These records do not prove that today's brand must fail. They do prove that
recognition and an asset-light model did not preserve economics through prior
demand slumps. Segment definitions, channel mix, products, and management changed,
so the periods are adverse-condition evidence rather than a directly comparable
margin series.[8][9]

The official all-teen favorite-footwear series shows the following rounded
shares:[11][12][13][14]

| Spring 2022 | 6 | 1% | 7,100 teens; average age 16.2. |

| Spring 2023 | 6 | 2% | Official full table. |
| Fall 2023 | 6 | 2% | 9,193 teens; average age 15.7. |

This is a small rise followed by a rounded plateau, not demonstrated erosion.
It is also not customer retention, willingness to pay, or the whole customer
base.

Spring 2024 surveyed 6,020 teens and disclosed only the top five footwear brands;
Fall 2024 surveyed 13,515 and disclosed only the top three; Fall 2025 surveyed
10,969 and disclosed only the top three. Crocs is not shown in those abbreviated
tables, so its exact rank/share is unknown rather than zero.[15][16][17]

A secondary Spring 2025 account says 12% of upper-income male teens ranked Crocs
No. 1 `on the way out` and 6% of upper-income female teens ranked it No. 4; it
reports prior Fall 2024 values of 10% and 5%. The official Spring 2025 table,
question wording, and income threshold were not recovered.[18] This concern is
relevant, but it is a different question and subgroup from all-teen favorite
footwear. Combining the two would create a false trend.

### Broader evidence confirms current relevance, not ten-year protection

AlixPartners' July 2024 online survey of 9,000 consumers ranked Crocs first among
fashion-footwear players across five retail pillars and first among the listed
fashion-footwear choices for Gen Z at 25% and Millennials at 29%.[19] StockX
ranked Crocs its No. 2 best-selling shoe brand in 2024 for the second consecutive
year.[20] YouGov's Q4 2025 U.S. measure reports 94% fame and 51% popularity; its
method accumulates 90 days of responses and weights the sample to be nationally
representative.[21][22] A focused account of Circana data says mules/clogs were
among the footwear styles that dominated Q4 2025 sales.[23]

These observations independently contradict a broad claim that Crocs was already
generally rejected. They do not measure repeat purchase, price elasticity,
constant-product premium, or Crocs-specific category share. StockX is a resale
marketplace; AlixPartners measures a multi-pillar retailer assessment; YouGov
measures opinion; and the Circana passage is category-level secondary access.
They cannot be averaged into a durability score.[2][24]

This result exposes two application errors beyond the score vector. Outsourced
production does not establish that structural cost advantage is absent; without
peer unit-cost and reproducibility evidence, it is Unknown. Likewise, no evidence
of a shared-scale loop supports Unknown, not Absent.[2][3] The result does not
show that Crocs has no brand moat. It shows that the current record cannot support
a ten-year durability rating or stable-trend statement.

### The record changes the report, not the framework

The published report already says fashion durability is the competence limit,
that independent demand evidence is thin, that pricing power is mixed, and that
past demand slumps produced losses. It nevertheless assigns Durability 3, Trend
3, and `Narrow / stable`.[3] The current framework already requires weak-period
review, independent corroboration, price-volume separation, N/A for unsupported
dimensions, and `Unclear` before the other classes.[2] The company template
already requires a limitation per category and directs unsupported case values
to N/A.[4]

The evidence bridge is therefore a bounded application record, not evidence for
a new scoring method, framework clause, or report field. It produces a
reproducible decision-relevant change from the published report, but no test
shows incremental classification value over correct use of the existing full
contract. Its 14,540-byte source pack and two detailed reader outputs also do not
establish lower review cost. No review-time comparator or prospective author
behavior was measured.

The moat correction has a bounded valuation implication. The current company
report's Base DCF assumes about USD720 million of owner cash held roughly flat,
and its thesis row says a Narrow moat fits the no-growth Base case.[3] `Unclear`
does not mechanically invalidate that scenario or recalculate intrinsic value;
it removes the moat classification as evidence that the persistence assumption
is supported. The scenario must remain explicitly conditional until durability
is resolved. This research does not issue a security recommendation, trade
instruction, or revised valuation.

For the current report, the supported factual implication is an uncertain moat
classification, not `None`. Protection 3 and Economic proof 3 remain defensible:
Crocs has meaningful preference and unusually high recent brand economics. The
unsupported step is converting those facts into Durability 3 and stable trend.
The economic consequence is conservative: a valuation may model durable cash as
a scenario, but cannot cite the current moat record as proof that the scenario
will persist.

A reusable method proposal is not supported by this run. The record did not
measure review cost, compare a smaller standardized bridge against an equally
informed full-contract reader, or test unseen company reports. The existing rule
was sufficient. Evaluation can separately decide whether a report-only proposal
is worth advancing. This research does not issue ADVANCE or edit Investing Hub.

## Response to Feedback and Remaining Questions

No prior evaluation exists for this pipeline. The first-report questions are
answered as follows:

1. **Does the full current contract support `Narrow / stable`?** No. Two isolated
   applications to the same frozen source record returned
   `3/3/N/A/N/A, Unclear / indeterminate, Low`. The exact outputs agree on every
   decision field.
2. **Can price be separated from volume and mix?** Partly. Annual units and
   nominal ASP identify direction, and issuer explanations identify price, mix,
   channel, and discounting contributions. No constant-product real-price series
   exists, so pricing power remains unproved.
3. **Does the consumer record establish erosion?** No. The all-teen favorite
   series rises from a rounded 1% to 2% and then plateaus through Fall 2023; later
   favorite tables omit Crocs, while the secondary `on the way out` result uses a
   different question and subgroup. Broader 2024-2025 evidence remains positive.
4. **Does a compact evidence-bridge method add value beyond the full rule?** Not
   established. The bridge changes the published application, but both readers
   reach the change by applying rules that already exist. Cost reduction and
   prospective error prevention are unknown.
5. **What remains blocking?** Customer retention and repeat-purchase cohorts;
   constant-product real price and elasticity; comparable brand share;
   Crocs Brand invested capital and after-reinvestment returns; current quarterly
   units/ASP/margin; and an adverse-cycle record under the current business mix.

A practical reassessment trigger is two consecutive quarters of falling Crocs
Brand revenue with company-attributed lower prices or increased discounting. If
quarterly ASP remains undisclosed, concurrent wholesale and DTC declines are a
weaker warning rather than proof. An annual combination of lower brand ASP and
lower brand operating margin is another observable warning. Evidence that would
resolve rather than merely warn includes independent retention/elasticity data,
a comparable market-share series, and segment economics sustained through a
weak demand period.

Confidence is low. Primary filings make the financial bridge strong, and two
preserved readers reproduce the current-rule result. Confidence remains low
because the conclusion-critical durability evidence is missing, the consumer
sources measure different populations and questions, historical periods are not
fully comparable, and both readers use one model family. Confidence would rise
if independent retention, constant-product pricing, comparable share, segment
returns, and adverse-cycle evidence converge. It would fall if the reconstructed
filing tables or preserved reader hashes cannot be reproduced.

## Economics, durability, attack, and stakeholder checks

- Pricing power: nominal ASP rose 9.7% in 2023 and 2.4% in 2024, but those changes mixed price, product, channel, geography, and discounting; ASP fell 0.5% with increased discounting in 2025, so constant-product real pricing power is unproved [P, 2022-2025 bridge; Crocs 2025 Form 10-K MD&A pp. 37 and 40].
- Current economics: Crocs Brand operating margin was 32.0%, 35.8%, 36.1%, and 33.4% in 2022-2025, but operating income fell 6.0% in 2025 [P, 2022-2025 bridge; arithmetic rechecked with `bc -l`].
- Reinvestment and returns: the allowed record does not provide Crocs Brand invested operating capital, cash generation after necessary reinvestment, or comparable peer returns, so no perimeter ROIC or capital-cost spread is forced [C, Business and Method; P, Evidence boundaries].
- Adverse conditions: pre-HEYDUDE company evidence, close but not identical to the perimeter, shows operating losses in 2008, 2014, 2015, and 2016, with discounting, liquidation, mix, and demand effects; changed segment and channel definitions limit comparability [P, Weak-period checks; Crocs 2009 and 2016 Forms 10-K].
- Well-funded attack: a rival could copy clog functionality, undercut price, buy retail visibility, and market substitutes; Crocs' recognition may slow customer capture, but no switching cost, protected supply, or measured retention prevents it [C, Moat; P, consumer record].
- Expansion versus protection: Q2 2026 brand growth and international expansion are opportunities, not evidence that existing economics are protected [P, Q2 2026 update].
- Stakeholders and ethics: the allowed evidence does not establish material customer, worker, supplier, or regulatory harm; 45% manufacturer concentration and duty exposure are economic dependencies, not demonstrated ethical moat erosion [C, Business and Method].
